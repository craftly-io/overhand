Now: one screen.

  • One sentence At the top left: how many agents are working, how many things need you, what merged this week and today's money. Each number opens what it counts: {{w.viewCrew}}, {{w.paneAlerts}} in full, {{w.viewWork}} or {{w.viewHealth}}.
  • {{w.paneAlerts}} One card for each thing that asks for you, with the button that settles it. A broken check or a new error, {{w.paneToday}} and the project agents worth starting arrive here too. With nothing waiting, only the sentence stays on the left.
  • {{w.paneInProgress}} On the right: every agent at work, task runs, project agents and your own sessions alike, then the pull requests they opened. Each row says its project, what it's doing and for how long, with Open; finished runs fold away below it. {{w.fieldHide}} folds it to a button.
  • The field On a computer, behind both lists: each project sits on the field under its name, the busiest today nearest the middle. Its agents hang off it on threads, and anything that waits on you leans out with an orange thread to its card. Settle the card and the thread lets go. Hover an agent for what it's doing; click it to open it. In every look but Instrument the field is that look's own world: Catnap's rug and cats, Observatory's sky and satellites, Transit's map and trains, Koi Pond's water and koi.
  • {{w.stripTitle}} Along the foot: the day, with busy hours given more room and quiet ones folded, your meetings above it and the day's events as marks. Drag along it, or {{w.stripReplay}}, to see the field as it was; {{w.stripLive}} returns.
  • Nothing scrolls Each list scrolls on its own; the page stays put. On a phone, Now is the sentence and the two lists, one under the other.
  • The views behind it {{w.viewWork}}, {{w.viewCrew}} and {{w.viewHealth}} open as drawers, from the sentence, the W, A and H keys or the command bar.
  • The header The status, {{w.briefMe}}, {{w.pauseShort}} with {{w.quiet}} inside it, the command bar and {{w.settings}}.

Focus: outcomes only.

  • What stays What needs you, what finished and what broke. What agents are saying, subagents and the day's in-between beats fold behind {{w.showSteps}}.
  • Turn it on The Focus switch in Settings → You, the F key, or focus in the command bar. Each browser keeps its own choice.
  • Its default On for a new install. An install in use before Focus existed starts with it off.

The menu bar item.

  • What it shows On a Mac, the Overhand mark in the menu bar, with the count of what needs you beside it: amber, or red when something is broken. Click it for who's at work, what needs you, {{w.quiet}}, {{w.glanceOpen}} and {{w.briefMe}}.
  • What a click opens A card or a sentence opens its place in the full dashboard, in your browser, signed in.
  • Turn it off {{w.glanceHide}} on it, or the {{w.menubar}} switch in Settings → You, which turns it back on. It comes with the installed Overhand on a Mac; a source checkout builds it with bun run menubar.

Looks.

  • Pick one Settings → You → {{w.theme}}, or theme in the command bar: Instrument (the default), Observatory, Transit, Koi Pond or Catnap.
  • What changes The colours, the greeting's face and a few of its lines. Every button, label and setting stays the same, in the same place.
  • The field Settings → You → {{w.sceneSetting}}: {{w.sceneOn}} (the default), {{w.sceneStill}} for one frame per change, or {{w.sceneOff}} for the plain lists.
  • Light and dark Each look follows your computer; {{w.appearance}} beside it picks light or dark instead.
  • Where it's kept In this browser, so other devices pick their own. The menu bar item draws in the look you picked last.

The night report.

  • Every morning One notification a day, from the hour you set in Settings → You: what merged and opened, what waits on you, and what yesterday was billed. With an ntfy topic it reaches your phone too.
  • {{w.briefMe}} In the header (beside Now's sentence on a phone), the actions menu or the command bar. It reads where things stand aloud and writes it down.
  • Stop While it reads, its button turns into Stop. Esc, closing the report or leaving the page stops it as well.

Share cards.

  • What it is {{w.share}} in the actions menu makes an image of what got done: the night, today or the week.
  • Where it goes Download it, or hand it to your device's share sheet where the browser offers one.

Status updates and work reports.

  • Chat updates Settings → Advanced → Status updates and work reports: each channel on Discord, Slack or Teams gets a summary of the projects you pick, on the schedule you pick. A test message checks it first.
  • Work reports Health → Work reports: per project, for the last week, month or any dates. Print it, or export Markdown or CSV. A weekly report can go to your channels too.

Suggest or report.

  • Where {{w.feedback}} in the actions menu, or feedback in the command bar.
  • You see it first It becomes a public GitHub issue, and the preview is exactly what goes. A diagnostics summary is yours to add. Anything private it spots stops the send until you edit it out.
  • Sent by you With gh signed in it posts as you; otherwise it opens on GitHub, filled in, for you to submit.

What's new.

  • The list {{w.whatsNew}} in the actions menu lists every feature, newest first, each with a link to its page here.
  • Tips When a feature is newer than what your browser has seen, one quiet line sits beside it: one a day at most, never on your first day, gone for good once you close it.
  • Off Settings → You → Tips about new features.
